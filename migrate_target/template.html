A researcher operating at the intersection of temporal data and digital decay. Specializing in the preservation of ephemeral network states.

The digital landscape is often perceived as permanent, yet it is arguably the most fragile medium ever created. My work focuses on the "Living Archive"—the concept that data should not just be stored, but maintained in a state of active interaction. Born in the late 20th century, my perspective is shaped by the transition from physical analog documents to the volatile bitstreams of the modern era.

settings_input_component

By applying principles of traditional archival science to the chaotic structure of the web, I develop frameworks for "Documentarianism." This methodology rejects the glossy, over-processed aesthetic of contemporary interfaces in favor of high-contrast, high-utility environments that mirror the permanence of lead-plate printing.

"Information is either present or hidden. There is no middle ground in the binary truth of the record."

Currently based in the digital diaspora, I consult for cultural institutions on the longevity of their interactive installations and digital collections. Each project is treated as a unique piece of ephemera—designed to be used, meant to be remembered, and built to survive the eventual heat death of the server.
